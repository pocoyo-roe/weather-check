    How the cove is exposed

    North Landing is a chalk inlet on the north side of Flamborough Head. Its headlands block anything from the south, east and west, so the sea only gets in through the mouth, which opens to the north. The shaded wedge on the compass is the open sector the planner treats as fully exposed. Outside it, exposure fades out over the next 40° on each side, because big North Sea swell from the north-east still wraps around the headland.

    Each hour is scored on four things:

    • Waves into the cove: wave height × how directly it points into the mouth.
    • Onshore wind: wind speed × the same exposure. Strong gusts from any direction also count.
    • Tide plus waves: predicted sea level above mean sea level, plus half the effective wave height as a rough allowance for wave run-up. High springs with a northerly sea is the worst case.
    • Rain: heavy showers mark an hour marginal or poor.

    All limits are adjustable in Settings. The tide limits in particular are a first guess: note the tide height on a day the beach gets covered and set the limit to match.
